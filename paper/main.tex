\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Purpose.} Mask synthesis for projection lithography (inverse lithography, ILT; source--mask optimization, SMO) is nonconvex, so a local optimum says nothing about the best achievable image. We provide solver-independent, checkable limits on printability.

\noindent\textbf{Methods.} For the Abbe/Hopkins model of a periodic cell---scalar or vector, with arbitrary aberrations and focus sets---the best achievable dark-to-bright intensity ratio $\rhostar$ (equivalently the maximum full-range exposure latitude of a threshold resist at a prescribed edge tolerance) is bounded by a semidefinite relaxation in the diffraction-order domain. Its dual is a certificate: nonnegative weights on dark and bright sample points such that one explicit matrix is positive semidefinite, verified independently of the solver by rebuilding the certificate matrix in 50-digit arithmetic from the model equations and factorizing it with a positive margin. We prove that the relaxation equals the unlimited-multiple-exposure problem, that the collected spectra of nonnegative one-dimensional masks form the Carath\'eodory--Toeplitz cone, that complex transmission cannot improve the relaxation under symmetric illumination, and that pixelation at or above the collected-order count leaves the bound unchanged. A branch-and-bound certifier (interval bounds of positive semidefinite forms, and an SDP-bounded variant with verified dual certificates) bounds the single-mask optimum.

\noindent\textbf{Results.} The bound reproduces the classical $k_1$ cutoffs for DUV and EUV and the two-beam closed form for dense lines. On \randNonDegN{} non-degenerate random one-dimensional targets (one near-degenerate instance is reported separately), local optimization is certified optimal to within $10^{-4}$ in \randExact{} of cases and to within 1\% in \randOne{}; all two-dimensional contact arrays are certified within \contactsGapMax{}, and the annular T-junction and line-end cells within \tjAnnGap{} and \leGap{}. Strict multi-exposure gains of \bnbGainLo--\bnbGainHi{} (random coherent targets, converged branch-and-bound) and \bnbMixGain{} (mixed-width target) are certified against the ratio realized by a feasible multi-exposure ensemble. The bound is \rayLo--\rayHi$\times$ tighter than the Rayleigh-quotient bound, brackets the penalty of pixelated nonnegative masks relative to phase-shift masks between certified lower bounds of \binPenLo--\binPenHi{} and upper bounds of \binPenHiLo--\binPenHiHi{}, and brackets the suboptimality of a standard pattern-error ILT at \iltPsmLo--\iltPsmHi{} above the phase-shift optimum.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusions.} The certificates make local optimality checkable, quantify the multi-exposure and phase-shift entitlement, and retain their structure under the vector model and moderate aberrations; their numerical values are entitlements of the thin-mask aerial-image model, necessary but not sufficient for printability.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} computational lithography; inverse lithography; source--mask optimization; partially coherent imaging; semidefinite relaxation; duality certificates; branch and bound; exposure latitude.

\section{Introduction}
Computational lithography synthesizes a mask (and a source) so that the projected aerial image prints a target layout. In the Abbe formulation the image is a weighted sum, over source points, of squared moduli of filtered mask spectra: it is \emph{quadratic} in the mask. ILT and SMO therefore optimize nonconvex functionals. IBM's wavefront-optimization work states that mask optimization ``tends inherently to be a problem with non-convex quadratic constraints'' \cite{rosenbluth2007}; recent work on learned ILT describes objectives ``that often trap optimization in poor local minima'' \cite{nvidia2026}; and reviews list local optimality among the open problems \cite{pang2021,yang2025}. What is missing is a \emph{certificate}: a proof, for a given target, illumination, numerical aperture and wavelength, that no mask can do better than a stated value. Such certificates (i)~separate unprintable layouts from unconverged optimizers, (ii)~benchmark ILT engines against the true optimum, (iii)~quantify the value of multiple exposure and of phase-shift versus nonnegative masks, (iv)~generalize the $k_1$ rule to arbitrary layouts, and (v)~localize the geometry that limits printability through the support of the dual weights.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item[(C1)] Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}: dual certificates of printability, valid for any mask class and for incoherent sums of masks, verified by one PSD test independent of the solver, re-derived exactly in 50-digit arithmetic from the model equations (\S\ref{sec:alg}).
\item[(C2)] Theorem~\ref{thm:me}: the relaxation equals the unlimited-multi-exposure problem, and Pataki's bound limits the number of useful exposures. Lemma~\ref{lem:complex}: complex transmission cannot improve the relaxation under symmetric illumination. Theorem~\ref{thm:toeplitz}: Carath\'eodory--Toeplitz realizability for nonnegative one-dimensional masks. Lemmas~\ref{lem:vector}--\ref{lem:pixel}: vector imaging, aberrations and pixelation preserve the structure.
\item[(C3)] Theorem~\ref{thm:bnb} and \S\ref{sec:alg}: branch-and-bound certificates of the single-mask optimum which, combined with (C1), separate single- from multi-exposure optima on concrete targets.
\item[(C4)] An open pipeline, an empirical study whose every number is generated from the result files, and independent verification of every certificate.
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{Not claimed.} No new SDP algorithm (the Shor relaxation of a QCQP is textbook \cite{luo2010,boyd2004}, and SDP-bounded branch-and-bound for nonconvex QCQPs is established \cite{burer2008}); no full-chip scalability; no thick-mask electromagnetics, resist chemistry or stochastics; no statement about proprietary ILT engines. ``Certified'' means exact in exact arithmetic and safeguarded, not interval-verified, against double-precision rounding (\S\ref{sec:alg}).

\paragraph{Organization.} \S\ref{sec:model} states the imaging model, the synthesis problem and its relaxation; \S\ref{sec:theory} proves the certificate, the multi-exposure identity, the Toeplitz characterization and the branch-and-bound bound; \S\ref{sec:alg} describes the solver, the round-off safeguard and the primal heuristics; \S\ref{sec:exp} reports the experiments; the Discussion and Limitations close the paper.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Wavefront optimization.} Rosenbluth et al.\ \cite{rosenbluth2002,rosenbluth2007} optimize the collected diffraction orders directly, maximizing exposure latitude subject to sampled bright/dark intensity constraints---the same objective as ours; a patent of the same group writes the problem as a quadratic program in the order amplitudes \cite{ibm_patents}, and the source step is a linear program \cite{rosenbluth2006}. Their global strategies partition the solution space into regions with at most one local minimum \cite{rosenbluth2007}, use dominant ``joint eigenvectors'' and a ``step-walk relaxation'', and, for multiple exposure, an unconstrained Rayleigh-quotient simplification \cite{ibm_patents}. None of these provides a certificate for the pointwise max-dark/min-bright ratio of a solution obtained by other means: the partition and step-walk strategies are search heuristics, and the multiple-exposure patent minimizes an \emph{averaged} dark-to-bright objective whose global minimum would bound the pointwise ratio only through that weaker averaged quantity; we compare with its Rayleigh-quotient bound in Table~\ref{tab:rayleigh}. The stacked pupil-shift matrix of \cite{yamazoe2008} is the channel matrix of our Eq.~\eqref{eq:image}; pixel-based ILT \cite{granik2006,poonawala2007} and SMO \cite{socha2005} are the local methods our certificates evaluate.
\paragraph{Quadratic programs and SDP in lithography.} Nonaka et al.\ \cite{nonaka2025} write pixel mask optimization as a 0--1 convex QCQP and relax it heuristically (no bound); Nouralishahi, Wu and Vandenberghe \cite{nouralishahi2008} use semidefinite programming for lithography \emph{model calibration} with a PSD imaging-kernel constraint, not for synthesis; PSD relaxations with randomized rounding serve the combinatorial mask-assignment problem of multiple patterning \cite{kohira2016}. Liu and Zakhor \cite{liu1990,liu1992} designed binary and phase-shift masks by branch and bound on a pixel least-squares criterion (combinatorial).
\paragraph{SDP relaxation of QCQPs.} The Shor relaxation and its approximation theory are surveyed in \cite{luo2010}; max-min multicast beamforming \cite{sidiropoulos2006} has exactly the structure of coherent-illumination mask synthesis. Pataki \cite{pataki1998} bounds the rank of extreme solutions; Lasserre's hierarchy \cite{lasserre2001} tightens bounds for small problems; SDP-bounded branch-and-bound solves nonconvex QPs globally to a tolerance \cite{burer2008}. The Toeplitz-positivity description of nonnegative trigonometric sequences is standard in filter design \cite{davidson2002,dumitrescu2007}.
\paragraph{Bounds in photonic design.} Angeris, Vu\v{c}kovi\'c and Boyd \cite{angeris2019,angeris2021} bound inverse-designed photonic devices by Lagrangian duality; our work is the imaging-side analogue.
\paragraph{Information-theoretic limits and lifting.} Information-theoretic limits \cite{ma2017,wang2018} bound OPC fidelity through mutual information; these are not layout-specific duality certificates. PhaseLift \cite{candes2013} lifts quadratic measurements for recovery; we lift for synthesis. The optimal coherent decomposition of partially coherent imaging is due to \cite{pati1994}.

\section{Model and Problem Statement}\label{sec:model}
\subsection{Imaging model}
Let $\Lambda=[0,L_1)\times\dots\times[0,L_d)$, $d\in\{1,2\}$, be a periodic cell and $m:\Lambda\to\C$ the mask transmission with Fourier coefficients $M_k=|\Lambda|^{-1}\int_\Lambda m(x)e^{-2\pi i f_k\cdot x}\,dx$, $f_k=(k_1/L_1,k_2/L_2)$ (for $d=1$, $f_k=(k/L,0)$: a one-dimensional mask is invariant along $y$, but the illumination is still two-dimensional). Illumination is a finite area-weighted quadrature of the illuminator: source points $s_j\in\R^2$ (direction cosines, $|s_j|\le\sigma\,\NA$) with weights $w_j>0$, $\sum_j w_j=1$; the illuminators used are listed in \S\ref{sec:exp}. In an image-side medium of index $n$ the pupil is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pupil}
P_z(g)=\mathbb{1}\{|g|\lambda\le\NA\}\,\exp\!\Big(i\tfrac{2\pi n z}{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{1-|g|^2\lambda^2/n^2}-1\big)+iW(g)\Big),
\end{equation}
with defocus $z$ and aberration phase $W$. Only the collected orders $K=\{k:|f_k|\le(\NA+\max_j|s_j|)/\lambda\}$ contribute; an order $k$ is discarded from $K$ only if neither $k$ nor $-k$ is brought into the pupil by any source point, so that $K=-K$ (needed for the real-mask structure of Lemma~\ref{lem:real}; a dead order whose mirror is collected stays in $K$ as an unconstrained coordinate tied to its mirror by $M_{-k}=\overline{M_k}$), and $F=|K|$; $M\in\C^F$ denotes $(M_k)_{k\in K}$. The Abbe image at $x$ and focus $z$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:image}
\I_{M,z}(x)=\sum_{j} w_j\Big|\sum_{k\in K}P_z(f_k+s_j/\lambda)\,M_k\,e^{2\pi i f_k\cdot x}\Big|^2 = M^{\mathsf H}Q_{x,z}M,\qquad Q_{x,z}=\sum_j w_j c_{j,x,z}c_{j,x,z}^{\mathsf H}\PSD .
\end{equation}
This is the scalar thin-mask model; the vector extension is Lemma~\ref{lem:vector}.

\subsection{Synthesis problem and relaxation}
A target is specified by finite sample sets $B$ (bright) and $D$ (dark), placed at $\pm\tau$ (edge tolerance) from every target edge plus interior points, and by a focus set $Z$; throughout, $\tau$ is 10\% of the smallest critical dimension of the target (one-dimensional samples sit exactly at $\pm\tau$; two-dimensional targets are unions of axis-aligned rectangles and their samples lie exactly on the two offset contours at distance $\tau$ from the boundary of the union, every $\SI{2}{nm}$ along the contour including the circular arcs around convex corners; the exact distance of every sample is asserted to be at least $\tau$). Mask classes: $\mathcal M_\C=\C^F$; real masks $\mathcal M_\R=\{M:M_{-k}=\overline{M_k}\}$ (continuous-tone transmission in $[-1,1]$, the class containing phase-shift masks); nonnegative masks $\mathcal M_+\subset\mathcal M_\R$ (binary-like masks; an attenuated phase-shift mask with negative transmission belongs to $\mathcal M_\R$). The dark-to-bright ratio
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho}
\rho(M)=\frac{\max_{(x,z)\in D\times Z}\I_{M,z}(x)}{\min_{(x,z)\in B\times Z}\I_{M,z}(x)}\qquad(\rho:=+\infty\ \text{if the denominator vanishes})
\end{equation}
is scale-invariant. For a threshold resist (threshold $\theta$, dose $E$) every bright sample prints and no dark sample prints iff $\theta/\beta\le E<\theta/\delta$ with $\beta=\min_{B\times Z}\I$, $\delta=\max_{D\times Z}\I$; the full-range dose latitude, window width over its centre dose, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:el}
\EL(\rho)=\frac{2(1-\rho)}{1+\rho},
\end{equation}
with one dose over the whole focus set (the half-range value $(1-\rho)/(1+\rho)$ equals the Michelson contrast of the two extreme samples). The synthesis problem and its relaxation are
\begin{align}
(\mathrm P_{\mathcal M})\quad & \rhostar(\mathcal M)=\inf_{M\in\mathcal M\setminus\{0\}}\rho(M)\nonumber\\
 &\hphantom{\rhostar(\mathcal M)}= \inf\big\{t:\ M^{\mathsf H}Q_dM\le t\ \ \forall d\in D\times Z,\ \ M^{\mathsf H}Q_bM\ge1\ \ \forall b\in B\times Z\big\},\\
(\mathrm R)\quad & t_R=\min\big\{t:\ \Tr(Q_dX)\le t\ \ \forall d\in D\times Z,\ \ \Tr(Q_bX)\ge1\ \ \forall b\in B\times Z,\nonumber\\
 &\hphantom{t_R=\min\big\{t:\ }X\PSD\ \ [\,X=\conj(\Pi X\Pi)\,]\big\},\label{eq:R}
\end{align}
where $\Pi$ is the order-flip permutation and the bracketed constraint selects real masks.

\section{Theory}\label{sec:theory}
\begin{theorem}[Certificate]\label{thm:cert}
Let $\mu\ge0$ on $D\times Z$ with $\sum\mu=1$, $\lambda\ge0$ on $B\times Z$, and $S(\mu,\lambda)=\sum_d\mu_dQ_d-\sum_b\lambda_bQ_b\PSD$. Then $\rho(M)\ge\sum_b\lambda_b$ for every $M\in\C^F\setminus\{0\}$ and for every incoherent sum of images $\sum_i\I_{M_i}$. For real masks it suffices that $\tilde S=\mathrm{Re}(U^{\mathsf H}SU)\PSD$ with $U$ a unitary basis of $\mathcal M_\R$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\beta=\min_{B\times Z}\I>0$ and $\delta=\max_{D\times Z}\I$. Then $\delta=\delta\sum_d\mu_d\ge\sum_d\mu_dM^{\mathsf H}Q_dM=M^{\mathsf H}SM+\sum_b\lambda_bM^{\mathsf H}Q_bM\ge\beta\sum_b\lambda_b$. For sums of images apply the same chain termwise. For $M=Ur$, $r\in\R^F$, $M^{\mathsf H}SM=r^{\mathsf T}\tilde Sr$.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[Real structure]\label{lem:real}
$J(M)=\conj(\Pi M)$ is an antilinear involution; $\mathcal M_\R=\mathrm{Fix}(J)$ is a real $F$-dimensional subspace on which the Hermitian form is real. A PSD $X$ satisfies $X=\conj(\Pi X\Pi)$ iff $XJ=JX$; then $X=UYU^{\mathsf H}$ with $Y\in\mathbb S^F_+$ and $\Tr(QX)=\Tr(\tilde QY)$, $\tilde Q=\mathrm{Re}(U^{\mathsf H}QU)$. Hence (R) for real masks is a real SDP of the same size.
\end{lemma}
\begin{theorem}[Relaxation equals unlimited multiple exposure]\label{thm:me}
For $\mathcal M\in\{\mathcal M_\C,\mathcal M_\R\}$, $t_R$ equals the optimal ratio of the summed image of any number of incoherently superposed exposures of masks in $\mathcal M$ printed with one common threshold. Assume every bright form is nonzero ($Q_b\ne0$ for all $b$; otherwise $\rhostar=t_R=+\infty$). Then (R) attains its minimum and $\rhostar(\mathcal M)$ is attained by a mask (Lemma~\ref{lem:attain}); $\rhostar=t_R$ iff (R) has a rank-one optimal solution; and an extreme optimal solution of (R) has $\rank X=r$ with $r(r+1)/2\le|D\times Z|+|B\times Z|$ for $\mathcal M_\R$ and $r^2\le|D\times Z|+|B\times Z|$ for $\mathcal M_\C$, so at most $r$ exposures are needed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$X\PSD\iff X=\sum_{i\le F}M_iM_i^{\mathsf H}$, and $\Tr(QX)=\sum_i M_i^{\mathsf H}QM_i$ is the summed image; Lemma~\ref{lem:real} gives real $M_i$ in the real-structured case. Attainment is Lemma~\ref{lem:attain}. If $\rhostar=t_R$, an optimal mask (which exists by the lemma) gives a rank-one optimal $X$; conversely a rank-one optimal $X=MM^{\mathsf H}$ is an optimal mask. The optimal face of (R) is a face of a feasible set that contains no line, so it has extreme points, which are extreme points of the feasible set. Writing the inequalities with slacks, (R) is a standard-form SDP with $m=|D\times Z|+|B\times Z|$ equalities and block-diagonal cone $(X,\text{slacks},t^+,t^-)$; Pataki's theorem \cite{pataki1998} bounds $\sum_{\text{blocks}}r_b(r_b+1)/2\le m$ for real symmetric blocks and $\sum_b r_b^2\le m$ for Hermitian blocks.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[Attainment]\label{lem:attain}
Let $\mathcal N=\bigcap_{d}\ker Q_d$ be the common dark null space, $P$ the orthogonal projector onto $\mathcal N^\perp$, $B_0=\{b:Q_b=PQ_bP\}$ the bright forms vanishing on $\mathcal N$ and $B_{\mathcal N}=B\setminus B_0$. Assume $Q_b\ne0$ for all $b$. Then (R) attains its minimum, and $\rhostar(\mathcal M)$ is attained, for $\mathcal M\in\{\mathcal M_\C,\mathcal M_\R\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $Q_d=PQ_dP$, every dark value depends on $X$ only through $PXP$, and so does every bright value with $b\in B_0$. (i) \emph{Reduction.} Let $(\mathrm R_0)$ be (R) restricted to $A\PSD$ supported on $\mathcal N^\perp$ with the bright constraints of $B_0$ only. If $X$ is feasible for (R) then $A=PXP$ is feasible for $(\mathrm R_0)$ with the same $t$. If $A$ is feasible for $(\mathrm R_0)$ then $X_c=A+c(I-P)\PSD$ has the same dark and $B_0$ values and $\Tr(Q_bX_c)=\Tr(Q_bA)+c\Tr((I-P)Q_b(I-P))$ with $\Tr((I-P)Q_b(I-P))>0$ for $b\in B_{\mathcal N}$, so $X_c$ is feasible for (R) for $c$ large. Hence both problems have the same value and attain together. (ii) \emph{$(\mathrm R_0)$ attains.} Its dual is $\max\sum_{b\in B_0}\lambda_b$ subject to $\sum_d\mu_d=1$, $\mu,\lambda\ge0$ and $\sum_d\mu_dQ_d-\sum_{B_0}\lambda_bQ_b\PSD$ on $\mathcal N^\perp$. By definition of $\mathcal N$, $\sum_dQ_d$ is positive definite on $\mathcal N^\perp$, so uniform $\mu$ and small positive $\lambda$ are strictly dual feasible; $(\mathrm R_0)$ is feasible ($A=cP$ with $c$ large, since $\Tr(Q_bP)=\Tr(Q_b)>0$ for $b\in B_0$); strict dual feasibility of a feasible SDP implies that the primal optimum is attained \cite{vandenberghe1996}. (iii) \emph{Single masks.} Write $M=u+v$ with $u\in\mathcal N^\perp$, $v\in\mathcal N$. Dark values and $B_0$ values depend only on $u$. For $v$ outside the finitely many proper subspaces $\ker((I-P)Q_b(I-P))\cap\mathcal N$, $b\in B_{\mathcal N}$, and $c\to\infty$, every bright value with $b\in B_{\mathcal N}$ of $u+cv$ tends to $+\infty$. If $B_0=\emptyset$, $M=v$ has zero dark and positive bright values, so $\rhostar=0$ is attained. Otherwise $\rho(u+v)\ge\rho_0(u):=\max_dQ_d[u]/\min_{B_0}Q_b[u]$ for every $v$, with equality for $v$ generic and $c$ large, so $\rhostar=\inf_{u\ne0}\rho_0(u)$. On the unit sphere of $\mathcal N^\perp$ the numerator of $\rho_0$ is continuous and positive and the denominator continuous and nonnegative, so $\rho_0$ is a lower semicontinuous map into $(0,+\infty]$ on a compact set and attains its infimum at some $u^\star$; then $M=u^\star+cv$ attains $\rhostar$. For $\mathcal M_\R$ the same argument applies to the real forms $\tilde Q$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:real}. (If $\mathcal N=\{0\}$ the sphere argument applies directly.)
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:me} models double or multiple exposure of one resist with a common development threshold; Boolean multi-patterning (litho--etch--litho--etch) is a different process and is not bounded by $t_R$. A gap between a locally optimized single mask and $t_R$ is an upper bound on the multi-exposure benefit; it proves a strict benefit only when a certified single-mask lower bound exceeds the ratio of a feasible multi-exposure ensemble (\S\ref{sec:alg}, Theorem~\ref{thm:bnb}).
\end{remark}
\begin{theorem}[Nonnegative masks in one dimension]\label{thm:toeplitz}
Let $d=1$, $K=\{-K_{\max},\dots,K_{\max}\}$, $T(M)=(M_{a-b})_{a,b=0}^{K_{\max}}$ the Hermitian Toeplitz matrix of $M\in\mathcal M_\R$, and let $\mathcal C_+$ denote the set of in-band coefficient vectors of bounded nonnegative masks. Then
\[\{M:T(M)\succ0\}\ \subset\ \mathcal C_+\ \subset\ \{M:T(M)\PSD\}=\{\text{in-band coefficients of nonnegative measures}\},\]
both inclusions are strict, $\overline{\mathcal C_+}=\{M:T(M)\PSD\}$, and the nonnegative-mask problem $\inf_{M\in\mathcal C_+}\rho(M)$ has the same value as its relaxation over $\{T(M)\PSD\}$ (the latter is an outer approximation; the former need not attain the value). If $T(M)\succ0$, $M$ is realized by a bounded, continuous, strictly positive mask.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Necessity: $v^{\mathsf H}T(M)v=|\Lambda|^{-1}\int m(x)|\sum_av_ae^{2\pi iax/L}|^2dx\ge0$ for a nonnegative function or measure. The equality on the right is the Carath\'eodory--Toeplitz theorem \cite{grenander1958,dumitrescu2007}. The right inclusion is strict: $M_0=M_{\pm1}=a>0$ gives $T(M)=a\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\PSD$, the moment matrix of the point mass $a\delta_0$, whereas a bounded nonnegative function with these coefficients would satisfy $\int_0^Lm(x)(1-\cos(2\pi x/L))\,dx=0$, forcing $m=0$ a.e.\ and contradicting $M_0=a>0$; singular $T(M)$ corresponds to atomic measures. For $T(M)\succ0$ the Levinson--Durbin recursion gives the maximum-entropy autoregressive extension $f(\theta)=\varsigma^2/|a(e^{i\theta})|^2$ (a rational density; $\varsigma^2$ is the innovation variance) with $a$ zero-free on the closed disk, matching $M_0,\dots,M_{K_{\max}}$; $f$ is continuous, bounded and bounded away from zero, and scaling it to $\max f=1$ does not change $\rho$: this proves the left inclusion, which is strict because $\mathcal C_+$ is not open: $M=0$ (the zero mask) lies in $\mathcal C_+$ but not in $\{T\succ0\}$. The positive-definite cone is dense in the semidefinite cone, so $\overline{\mathcal C_+}=\{T\PSD\}$. Equality of the optimal values: for any $M$ with $T(M)\PSD$ and $\rho(M)<\infty$ (positive bright intensities), $M_\varepsilon=M+\varepsilon e_0$ satisfies $T(M_\varepsilon)=T(M)+\varepsilon I\succ0$, so $M_\varepsilon\in\mathcal C_+$, and $\rho(M_\varepsilon)\to\rho(M)$ by continuity of $\rho$ at points with positive bright intensities.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
In two dimensions block-Toeplitz positivity is necessary but not sufficient for the truncated trigonometric moment problem \cite{dumitrescu2007}; it still yields a valid relaxation.
\end{remark}
\begin{proposition}[Cutoff]\label{prop:cutoff}
If for every source point at most one collected order passes the pupil, then $Q_{x,z}$ is independent of $x$, $t_R=1$, and uniform $(\mu,\lambda)$ is a certificate. For $d=1$ the order $k$ passes for the source point $s_j=(s_{jx},s_{jy})$ iff $|k/L+s_{jx}/\lambda|\le\sqrt{\NA^2-s_{jy}^2}/\lambda$, so the hypothesis holds iff no two integers lie in any of the intervals $[-s_{jx}-\sqrt{\NA^2-s_{jy}^2},\,-s_{jx}+\sqrt{\NA^2-s_{jy}^2}]\,L/\lambda$; a sufficient condition, under which only the zero order passes, is $L\max_j\big(|s_{jx}|+\sqrt{\NA^2-s_{jy}^2}\big)<\lambda$, which for a disk illuminator of partial coherence $\sigma$ is the classical cutoff $L<\lambda/((1+\sigma)\NA)$. For $d=2$ a sufficient condition is $\max(L_1,L_2)(\NA+\max_j|s_j|)<\lambda$. The radial condition $L(\NA+\max_j|s_j|)<\lambda$ is sufficient but not necessary in general (e.g.\ $\NA=\lambda=1$, $L=2/3$, $s=(0,0.7)$: only the zero order passes although $L(\NA+|s|)=1.13$).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
With a single order per source point the image is $\sum_jw_j|M_{k_j}|^2$, independent of $x$; uniform weights give $S=0$ and $\sum_b\lambda_b=1$.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
The converse fails for some targets: just above the cutoff only the orders $0,\pm1$ interfere and the image is a sinusoid at the cell period, so a target whose bright and dark samples cannot be separated by such a sinusoid (e.g.\ $B=\{0,L/2\}$, $D=\{L/4,3L/4\}$) still has $t_R=1$. For dense lines $t_R<1$ immediately above the cutoff, and for two-beam imaging of dense lines at pitch $p$ the bound equals the closed form $(1-\sin(2\pi\tau/p))/(1+\sin(2\pi\tau/p))$ (verified to $2\times10^{-5}$ in the test suite).
\end{remark}
\begin{lemma}[Complex versus real masks]\label{lem:complex}
Let $w(-s)=w(s)$ and $P_0(-g)=\overline{P_0(g)}$ (even pupil amplitude, odd aberration phase). Then $\tilde M=\conj(\Pi M)$ produces the same image as $M$ at best focus, and with defocus $\I_{\tilde M,z}=\I_{M,-z}$. Consequently, for a focus set with $Z=-Z$ and no even aberrations, $t_R(\mathcal M_\C)=t_R(\mathcal M_\R)$ (take $X=\tfrac12(MM^{\mathsf H}+\tilde M\tilde M^{\mathsf H})$). Even aberrations (astigmatism, spherical, $Z_4$) break the identity.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Substituting $k\to-k$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:image} and using $\overline{P_z(-g)}=P_{-z}(g)$ and $w(-s)=w(s)$ gives $\I_{\tilde M,z}(x)=\I_{M,-z}(x)$; $X$ is real-structured since $\conj(\Pi MM^{\mathsf H}\Pi)=\tilde M\tilde M^{\mathsf H}$, and its constraint values are averages over $z\in Z=-Z$.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[Vector imaging and aberrations]\label{lem:vector}
With the image-side plane wave of direction cosines $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ carrying the mask-side polarization $p$ rotated by the minimal rotation taking $\hat z$ to the propagation direction (for $p=\hat x$: $E=(1-\alpha^2/(1+\gamma),\,-\alpha\beta/(1+\gamma),\,-\alpha)$, Rodrigues' formula; cf.\ \cite{flagello1996}), the image is a sum of rank-one Hermitian forms over channels (source point, polarization, field component), so Theorems~\ref{thm:cert}--\ref{thm:me} apply verbatim; Lemma~\ref{lem:complex} extends to reflection-symmetric polarization ensembles since $E_{x,y}(-g)=E_{x,y}(g)$, $E_z(-g)=-E_z(g)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{lemma}[Pixelation]\label{lem:pixel}
For a mask piecewise constant on $N\ge F$ pixels per period (one dimension; pixel $n$ covers $[nL/N,(n+1)L/N)$), $M_k=\mathrm{sinc}(k/N)\,e^{-i\pi k/N}\,\mathrm{DFT}_k(m)$ maps onto $\C^F$, so the pixel-domain and Fourier-domain relaxations coincide for the scale-free ratio problem; $N<F$ can only raise the bound.
\end{lemma}
\begin{remark}[Sampled versus continuous constraints]
The continuous problem has more constraints than the sampled one, so a certificate for the sampled problem is a valid lower bound for the continuous problem; tightness is a separate question. Numerically the interior samples carry zero dual weight and the bound is invariant to the interior spacing (\S\ref{sec:robust}).
\end{remark}
\begin{theorem}[Single-mask lower bound by branch and bound]\label{thm:bnb}
Let $Q_1,\dots,Q_m\PSD$ (dark) and $P_1,\dots,P_n\PSD$ (bright) be real symmetric and $\rho(r)=\max_iq_i(r)/\min_jp_j(r)$. (i) $\rhostar(\mathcal M_\R)=\inf$ over the faces $\Phi_i=\{r:r_i=1,|r_j|\le1\}$. (ii) For a box $\{c+\delta:|\delta_j|\le h_j\}$ and $q(r)=r^{\mathsf T}Qr$ with $Q\PSD$: $q(c)-2|Qc|\cdot h\le q(r)\le q(c)+2|Qc|\cdot h+h^{\mathsf T}|Q|h$. (iii) Hence $\inf_{\text{box}}\rho\ge\mathrm{LB}:=\max_i\max(q_i(c)-2|Q_ic|\cdot h,0)/\min_j(p_j(c)+2|P_jc|\cdot h+h^{\mathsf T}|P_j|h)$, and a branch-and-bound over boxes covering $\bigcup_i\Phi_i$ returns, at any stopping time, a valid lower bound $L=\min_{\text{leaves}}\mathrm{LB}\le\rhostar(\mathcal M_\R)$ (leaves include the still-open boxes); on convergence $L\ge(1-\mathrm{tol})\,U$ with $U$ the incumbent.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) Scale and sign invariance of $\rho$: every $r\ne0$ has a coordinate of maximal modulus, which can be scaled to $+1$. (ii) $q(c+\delta)=q(c)+2(Qc)\cdot\delta+\delta^{\mathsf T}Q\delta$ with $0\le\delta^{\mathsf T}Q\delta\le h^{\mathsf T}|Q|h$. (iii) Interval arithmetic and the covering argument.
\end{proof}

\section{Algorithms}\label{sec:alg}
\paragraph{Bound.} The source quadrature is first compressed exactly into at most $F$ coherent channels by the eigendecomposition of the $F\times F$ transmission-cross-coefficient matrix $T_z=\sum_jw_jP_z(f+s_j/\lambda)P_z(f+s_j/\lambda)^{\mathsf H}$ (the sum-of-coherent-systems decomposition \cite{pati1994}; images agree to $10^{-15}$), then $Q_{x,z}$ is built on the edge-offset sample set and the real SDP is solved (CLARABEL \cite{clarabel}; SCS \cite{scs} as fallback).
\paragraph{Rigorization and exact verification.} Extract the duals $(\mu,\lambda)$ and find by bisection in double precision the largest $\kappa\le1$ with $\lambda_{\min}(S_D-\kappa S_B)\ge10^{-10}\|S_D-\kappa S_B\|$, $S_D=\sum_d\mu_dQ_d$, $S_B=\sum_b\lambda_bQ_b$ (a heuristic safeguard: the margin is relative to a difference that can suffer cancellation, and the forms $Q$ carry rounding errors of order $10^{-15}$). The reported bound is $\kappa\sum_b\lambda_b$. The certificate is then verified independently of this computation: the matrix $\tilde S=\mathrm{Re}\,U^{\mathsf H}(S_D-\kappa S_B)U$ is \emph{rebuilt} in 50-digit arithmetic from the model equations \eqref{eq:pupil}--\eqref{eq:image} and the instance data (cell, wavelength, $\NA$, index, focus set, source points and weights, sample coordinates; all exact as the double-precision numbers that define the instance), using the same $(\mu,\lambda,\kappa)$, and $\tilde S-10^{-30}I$ is Cholesky-factorized at 50 digits. This proves $\lambda_{\min}(\tilde S)\ge10^{-30}$ up to the 50-digit rounding error ($\lesssim F^2\,10^{-48}\|\tilde S\|$), hence Theorem~\ref{thm:cert} applies to the stated discrete instance. The minimum eigenvalue of the rebuilt matrix is reported with each certificate (\S\ref{sec:robust}). What is not proved is a bound for a \emph{different} discretization (finer source quadrature, other sample points), and no interval arithmetic is used.
\paragraph{Primal.} Gaussian rounding from $X$; analytic-gradient L-BFGS on a log-sum-exp surrogate of $\log\rho$ (common dose across foci); vectorized SLSQP minimax polish; multistart; optimal-face exploration (random PSD objectives over the optimal face) for low-rank optima; explicit two-exposure refinement.
\paragraph{Single-mask certification.} (a) Theorem~\ref{thm:bnb} with batched boxes; error terms are multiplied by $1+10^{-9}$ and $10^{-12}$ is subtracted (a heuristic safeguard, not a proof of rounding control). (b) An SDP-bounded variant: to certify $\rhostar(\mathcal M_\R)\ge T$ the set $\{r\in\text{box}:\ r^{\mathsf T}(Q_d-TP_b)r\le0\ \forall(d,b)\}$ is shown empty through the Shor relaxation $[1,r^{\mathsf T};r,X]\PSD$ with reformulation--linearization box cuts, solved as ``minimize the common slack''; each node is pruned only if an explicit dual certificate $(y\ge0,\ \alpha,\ \beta)$ with $\sum_m y_mG_m+\alpha E_{00}+\beta E_{ii}\PSD$ and $\sum_my_mc_m-\alpha-\beta>0$ is verified after the solve (weak duality), so solver inaccuracy cannot produce a false pruning; restricting the pairs $(d,b)$ to the active supports of the global certificate keeps the relaxation valid. (c) Lasserre-2 moment relaxations \cite{lasserre2001} are reported as corroboration only (solver tolerance limited).
\paragraph{Variants.} SMO relaxation (one PSD block per source point: unlimited exposures with coherent point sources); doubly-nonnegative (DNN) relaxation for nonnegative masks on a pixel grid; complex-mask relaxation; multi-focus; vector and aberrated pupils; pixel-integrated masks.
\paragraph{Complexity.} $F\approx(2(1+\sigma)\NA L/\lambda)^d$; the SDP has an $F\times F$ matrix variable and $|B|+|D|$ constraints per focus. Solve times are below \sdpTimeSmallHi\,s for $F\le15$ and \sdpTimeBigLo--\sdpTimeBigHi\,s for $F=\tjF$ with \tjConstraints{} constraints.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}
\paragraph{Settings.} DUV: $\lambda=\SI{193}{nm}$, $\NA=1.35$, defocus in water ($n=1.44$); EUV: $\lambda=\SI{13.5}{nm}$, $\NA=0.33$, vacuum. Illuminators are area-weighted midpoint-grid quadratures of the illuminator aperture on a $401\times401$ grid over its bounding square: coherent (one on-axis point); conventional $\sigma\in\{0.5,0.7,0.9\}$ (disk; \quadNconv{} points for $\sigma=0.7$); dipole $\sigma_c\in\{0.7,0.8\}$ (two circular poles of radius $0.15\,\NA$ on the $x$ axis, $121\times121$ grid per pole); annular 0.6/0.9 (DUV) and 0.5/0.9 (EUV); quasar (four $30^\circ$ sectors of the annulus). The per-source SMO relaxation uses a coarse $21\times21$ rule (349 points), since it carries one matrix variable per source point. Edge tolerance $\tau$ is 10\% of the minimum CD (\SI{5}{nm} for the \SI{50}{nm} one-dimensional features). ``Bound'' is always the rigorized certificate value and ``best single mask'' the best feasible real mask found (\S\ref{sec:notation}); EL is the full-range value \eqref{eq:el}. \subsection{Adversarial one-dimensional targets}\label{sec:adv}
Near-cutoff mixed pitches, SRAF-like \SI{20}{nm} spaces, 5\%/95\% duty cycles, alternating widths and three-pitch mixtures (Fig.~\ref{fig:dataset}, middle; $\tau=10\%$ CD) are tight under dipole illumination, mostly tight under the $\sigma=0.7$ disk, and show gaps of \advCohGapLo--\advCohGapHi{} in \advCohBigN{} of six coherent cases, the regime in which multiple exposure helps most. The isolated \SI{20}{nm} space under coherent or dipole light has a certified bound of $\approx0$: a phase-edge mask makes the sampled dark points exactly dark. This is a statement about the sampled constraint set, not about the printed width of a phase-edge line, which is dose-determined.

\subsection{Robustness, solver validation and physical sensitivity}\label{sec:robust}
The bound is invariant to the interior sample spacing (\spacingMin--\spacingMax{} for spacings of 16 to \SI{1}{nm}) and monotone in $\tau$ (full-range EL: \marginList). Quadrature convergence of the $\sigma=0.7$ disk (iso-dense target): \quadList{} (the $401\times401$ rule used throughout differs from the finest, $801\times801$, by \quadRefDiff). This residual is a discretization uncertainty that solver certification does not cover: every certificate is a statement about the stated discrete illuminator, sample set and focus set, and only an estimate, not a bound, is available for the continuous illuminator. Because the continuous printing problem has more constraints than the sampled one, a bound for the sampled problem remains a valid bound for the continuous geometry under the same illuminator, but the tightness statements (best masks) hold only for the sampled constraints: zero dual weight on interior samples does not exclude violations between samples. The Rayleigh-quotient bound is \rayLo--\rayHi$\times$ weaker (Table~\ref{tab:rayleigh}), and complex- and real-mask relaxations coincide to $\cplxMaxDiff$ on all \rayN{} cases (Lemma~\ref{lem:complex}). Every record of every experiment satisfies best single mask $\ge$ bound (\certViolations{} violations), the consistency check that an invalid certificate would fail. All \verN{} bounds of the verification set were recomputed in double precision and their computed certificate matrices factorized by a 50-digit Cholesky (\verChol{} succeed, \verDeg{} degenerate with $S=0$, \verFail{} fail). Independently, for \exactN{} of these instances the certificate matrix was rebuilt in 50-digit arithmetic from the model equations (\S\ref{sec:alg}): \exactPD{} are positive definite with margin $10^{-30}$ (minimum eigenvalue of the rebuilt matrix between $\exactLminLo$ and $\exactLminHi$), \exactZero{} are zero certificates, \exactFail{} fail; for \exactBisected{} instance(s) the double-precision scale $\kappa$ was not safe for the exact matrix (a near-zero certificate at the EUV cutoff) and $\kappa$ was lowered by an exact bisection at 50 digits, changing the bound by less than $10^{-15}$. The reported bounds are the exactly verified values; on the \verNonDegN{} non-degenerate instances the rigorization scale is at least \verKappaMin{} and below $0.9999$ in \verKappaBelow{} of them; on the near-degenerate random instance the interior-point dual is unusable and the certificate is scaled down to $\verDegKappa$ (a valid, nearly vacuous bound), which the local search confirms by reaching $\rho\le\randDegBestMax$. CLARABEL and SCS agree to a median of $\verSolverMedian$; on \verBigDiffN{} near-degenerate instances (bounds up to \verBigDiffBoundMax) the first-order solver SCS is off by up to \verBigDiffMax, which the certificate check exposes. Under the vector model ($n_{\mathrm{resist}}=1.7$, Fig.~\ref{fig:bnb}, right) the one-dimensional TE bounds change by \vecTeLo{} to \vecTeHi{}, the unpolarized bounds by \vecUnLo{} to \vecUnHi{} and the TM bounds by \vecTmLo{} to \vecTmHi{} relative to the scalar model (the classical TM contrast loss at $\NA=1.35$), with gaps at most \vecGapMax; on the two-dimensional contacts the unpolarized bounds rise by \vecTwoDUnLo--\vecTwoDUnHi{} and the TE bounds by \vecTwoDTeLo--\vecTwoDTeHi. The vector model omits Fresnel transmission and thin-film effects, which rescale channel amplitudes without changing the structure of Lemma~\ref{lem:vector}. Aberrations of 0.02--0.1$\lambda$ move the iso-dense bound by up to \abMax; pixelation at $N\ge F$ leaves it unchanged to $\pixSameMaxDev$ and coarser pixels raise it (\pixCoarse), as Lemma~\ref{lem:pixel} predicts.

\section{Discussion}
The certificates turn folklore into checkable statements. Local optimization of the exposure-latitude objective is certified near-optimal (within $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-2}$) for most one-dimensional targets and for the two-dimensional contact arrays. When a gap remains, Theorem~\ref{thm:me} identifies $t_R$ with the multi-exposure optimum but not the local-search result with the single-mask optimum: the gap is an upper bound on the multi-exposure benefit that may also contain optimizer shortfall. A strict benefit is established only where a certified single-mask lower bound exceeds the ratio of a feasible ensemble, which the converged branch-and-bound runs do on concrete coherent-illumination targets; the unresolved gaps (quasar T-junction, large-$F$ random layouts) are not evidence either way. On-axis coherent light with real masks is the multi-exposure entitlement for the iso-dense target, and nonnegative masks forfeit a certified \binPenLo--\binPenHi{} (at most \binPenHiLo--\binPenHiHi). Pattern-error ILT is far from EL-optimal, which argues for EL or contrast objectives, or for the certificate as a stopping criterion. The dual weights localize the limiting geometry. The structural conclusions survive the vector model and moderate aberrations; the numerical entitlements depend on the illuminator quadrature, the defocus medium and the edge tolerance, all of which are stated with each table. Finally, the relaxation gap has a physical meaning and is informative rather than merely a weakness.

\section{Limitations}
The model is a thin-mask periodic model: no three-dimensional mask electromagnetics (shadowing and best-focus shifts in EUV), no resist chemistry or stochastics, no flare, no mask error, and no dose or MEEF constraint (the ratio is scale-free; a dose-aware variant with $\Tr X\le1$ remains an SDP). Constraints are sampled with an explicit edge tolerance, and the EL objective fixes one threshold across the focus set; constraints linear in intensity (slope, dose-to-size) lift identically, but two-sided CD constraints and Boolean multi-patterning do not. The DNN relaxation is only an outer approximation in two dimensions and applies to pixelated nonnegative masks. ``Certified'' means: exact in exact arithmetic and verified in 50-digit arithmetic from the model equations for the stated discrete instance (no interval arithmetic, and no statement about other discretizations of the illuminator or of the geometry). The interval branch-and-bound converges only for $F=5$ and is informative to $F=7$; the SDP-bounded variant certifies gains only away from the incumbent; one \SI{320}{nm} two-dimensional gap and the large-$F$ random two-dimensional gaps remain unresolved. Near-degenerate instances stress first-order solvers. Literature verification relied on abstracts and search snippets; the full texts of \cite{rosenbluth2002,rosenbluth2007,liu1992,nonaka2025,nouralishahi2008} were not accessible, so overlap with the body of those works cannot be excluded.
