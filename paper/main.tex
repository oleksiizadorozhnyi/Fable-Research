\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Purpose.} Mask synthesis for projection lithography (inverse lithography, ILT; source--mask optimization, SMO) is nonconvex, so a local optimum says nothing about the best achievable image. We provide solver-independent, checkable limits on printability.

\noindent\textbf{Methods.} For the Abbe/Hopkins model of a periodic cell---scalar or vector, with arbitrary aberrations and focus sets---the best achievable dark-to-bright intensity ratio $\rhostar$ (equivalently the maximum full-range exposure latitude of a threshold resist at a prescribed edge tolerance) is bounded by a semidefinite relaxation in the diffraction-order domain. Its dual is a certificate: nonnegative weights on dark and bright sample points such that one explicit matrix is positive semidefinite, verified independently of the solver and made safe against round-off. We prove that the relaxation equals the unlimited-multiple-exposure problem, that the collected spectra of nonnegative one-dimensional masks form the Carath\'eodory--Toeplitz cone, that complex transmission cannot improve the relaxation under symmetric illumination, and that pixelation at or above the collected-order count leaves the bound unchanged. A branch-and-bound certifier (interval bounds of positive semidefinite forms, and an SDP-bounded variant with verified dual certificates) bounds the single-mask optimum.

\noindent\textbf{Results.} The bound reproduces the classical $k_1$ cutoffs for DUV and EUV and the two-beam closed form for dense lines. On \randN{} random one-dimensional targets, local optimization is certified globally optimal in \randExact{} of cases and within 1\% in \randOne{}; all two-dimensional contact arrays are certified within \contactsGapMax{}, and the annular T-junction and line-end cells within \tjAnnGap{} and \leGap{}. Multi-exposure gains of \bnbGainLo--\bnbGainHi{} (random coherent targets, converged branch-and-bound) and \bnbMixGain{} (mixed-width target) are certified up to floating-point rounding. The bound is \rayLo--\rayHi$\times$ tighter than the Rayleigh-quotient bound, certifies that pixelated nonnegative masks forfeit \binPenLo--\binPenHi{} relative to phase-shift masks, and shows that a standard pattern-error ILT lands \iltPsmLo--\iltPsmHi{} above the certified optimum.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusions.} The certificates make local optimality checkable, quantify the multi-exposure and phase-shift entitlement, and retain their structure under the vector model and moderate aberrations; their numerical values are entitlements of the thin-mask aerial-image model, necessary but not sufficient for printability.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Computational lithography synthesizes a mask (and a source) so that the projected aerial image prints a target layout. In the Abbe formulation the image is a weighted sum, over source points, of squared moduli of filtered mask spectra: it is \emph{quadratic} in the mask. ILT and SMO therefore optimize nonconvex functionals. IBM's wavefront-optimization work states that mask optimization ``tends inherently to be a problem with non-convex quadratic constraints'' \cite{rosenbluth2007}; recent work on learned ILT describes objectives ``that often trap optimization in poor local minima'' \cite{nvidia2026}; and reviews list local optimality among the open problems \cite{pang2021,yang2025}. What is missing is a \emph{certificate}: a proof, for a given target, illumination, numerical aperture and wavelength, that no mask can do better than a stated value. Such certificates (i)~separate unprintable layouts from unconverged optimizers, (ii)~benchmark ILT engines against the true optimum, (iii)~quantify the value of multiple exposure and of phase-shift versus nonnegative masks, (iv)~generalize the $k_1$ rule to arbitrary layouts, and (v)~localize the geometry that limits printability through the support of the dual weights.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item[(C1)] Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}: dual certificates of printability, valid for any mask class and for incoherent sums of masks, verified by one PSD test independent of the solver, together with a round-off-safe rigorization (\S\ref{sec:alg}).
\item[(C2)] Theorem~\ref{thm:me}: the relaxation equals the unlimited-multi-exposure problem, and Pataki's bound limits the number of useful exposures. Lemma~\ref{lem:complex}: complex transmission cannot improve the relaxation under symmetric illumination. Theorem~\ref{thm:toeplitz}: Carath\'eodory--Toeplitz realizability for nonnegative one-dimensional masks. Lemmas~\ref{lem:vector}--\ref{lem:pixel}: vector imaging, aberrations and pixelation preserve the structure.
\item[(C3)] Theorem~\ref{thm:bnb} and \S\ref{sec:alg}: branch-and-bound certificates of the single-mask optimum which, combined with (C1), separate single- from multi-exposure optima on concrete targets.
\item[(C4)] An open pipeline, an empirical study whose every number is generated from the result files, and independent verification of every certificate.
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{Not claimed.} No new SDP algorithm (the Shor relaxation of a QCQP is textbook \cite{luo2010,boyd2004}, and SDP-bounded branch-and-bound for nonconvex QCQPs is established \cite{burer2008}); no full-chip scalability; no thick-mask electromagnetics, resist chemistry or stochastics; no statement about proprietary ILT engines. ``Certified'' means exact in exact arithmetic and safeguarded, not interval-verified, against double-precision rounding (\S\ref{sec:alg}).

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Wavefront optimization.} Rosenbluth et al.\ \cite{rosenbluth2002,rosenbluth2007} optimize the collected diffraction orders directly, maximizing exposure latitude subject to sampled bright/dark intensity constraints---the same objective as ours; a patent of the same group writes the problem as a quadratic program in the order amplitudes \cite{ibm_patents}, and the source step is a linear program \cite{rosenbluth2006}. Their global strategies partition the solution space into regions with at most one local minimum \cite{rosenbluth2007}, use dominant ``joint eigenvectors'' and a ``step-walk relaxation'', and, for multiple exposure, an unconstrained Rayleigh-quotient simplification \cite{ibm_patents}. None yields a bound on solutions obtained by other means; we compare with the Rayleigh-quotient bound in Table~\ref{tab:rayleigh}. The stacked pupil-shift matrix of \cite{yamazoe2008} is the channel matrix of our Eq.~\eqref{eq:image}; pixel-based ILT \cite{granik2006,poonawala2007} and SMO \cite{socha2005} are the local methods our certificates evaluate.
\paragraph{Quadratic programs and SDP in lithography.} Nonaka et al.\ \cite{nonaka2025} write pixel mask optimization as a 0--1 convex QCQP and relax it heuristically (no bound); Nouralishahi, Wu and Vandenberghe \cite{nouralishahi2008} use semidefinite programming for lithography \emph{model calibration} with a PSD imaging-kernel constraint, not for synthesis; PSD relaxations with randomized rounding serve the combinatorial mask-assignment problem of multiple patterning \cite{kohira2016}. Liu and Zakhor \cite{liu1990,liu1992} designed binary and phase-shift masks by branch and bound on a pixel least-squares criterion (combinatorial).
\paragraph{SDP relaxation of QCQPs.} The Shor relaxation and its approximation theory are surveyed in \cite{luo2010}; max-min multicast beamforming \cite{sidiropoulos2006} has exactly the structure of coherent-illumination mask synthesis. Pataki \cite{pataki1998} bounds the rank of extreme solutions; Lasserre's hierarchy \cite{lasserre2001} tightens bounds for small problems; SDP-bounded branch-and-bound solves nonconvex QPs globally to a tolerance \cite{burer2008}. The Toeplitz-positivity description of nonnegative trigonometric sequences is standard in filter design \cite{davidson2002,dumitrescu2007}.
\paragraph{Bounds in photonic design.} Angeris, Vu\v{c}kovi\'c and Boyd \cite{angeris2019,angeris2021} bound inverse-designed photonic devices by Lagrangian duality; our work is the imaging-side analogue.
\paragraph{Information-theoretic limits and lifting.} Information-theoretic limits \cite{ma2017,wang2018} bound OPC fidelity through mutual information; these are not layout-specific duality certificates. PhaseLift \cite{candes2013} lifts quadratic measurements for recovery; we lift for synthesis. The optimal coherent decomposition of partially coherent imaging is due to \cite{pati1994}.

\section{Model and Problem Statement}\label{sec:model}
\subsection{Imaging model}
Let $\Lambda=[0,L_1)\times\dots\times[0,L_d)$, $d\in\{1,2\}$, be a periodic cell and $m:\Lambda\to\C$ the mask transmission with Fourier coefficients $M_k=|\Lambda|^{-1}\int_\Lambda m(x)e^{-2\pi i f_k\cdot x}\,dx$, $f_k=(k_1/L_1,k_2/L_2)$ (for $d=1$, $f_k=(k/L,0)$: a one-dimensional mask is invariant along $y$, but the illumination is still two-dimensional). Illumination is a finite area-weighted quadrature of the illuminator: source points $s_j\in\R^2$ (direction cosines, $|s_j|\le\sigma\,\NA$) with weights $w_j>0$, $\sum_j w_j=1$; the illuminators used are listed in \S\ref{sec:exp}. In an image-side medium of index $n$ the pupil is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pupil}
P_z(g)=\mathbb{1}\{|g|\lambda\le\NA\}\,\exp\!\Big(i\tfrac{2\pi n z}{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{1-|g|^2\lambda^2/n^2}-1\big)+iW(g)\Big),
\end{equation}
with defocus $z$ and aberration phase $W$. Only the collected orders $K=\{k:|f_k|\le(\NA+\max_j|s_j|)/\lambda\}$, $F=|K|$, contribute; $M\in\C^F$ denotes $(M_k)_{k\in K}$. The Abbe image at $x$ and focus $z$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:image}
\I_{M,z}(x)=\sum_{j} w_j\Big|\sum_{k\in K}P_z(f_k+s_j/\lambda)\,M_k\,e^{2\pi i f_k\cdot x}\Big|^2 = M^{\mathsf H}Q_{x,z}M,\qquad Q_{x,z}=\sum_j w_j c_{j,x,z}c_{j,x,z}^{\mathsf H}\PSD .
\end{equation}
This is the scalar thin-mask model; the vector extension is Lemma~\ref{lem:vector}.

\subsection{Synthesis problem and relaxation}
A target is specified by finite sample sets $B$ (bright) and $D$ (dark), placed at $\pm\tau$ (edge tolerance) from every target edge plus interior points, and by a focus set $Z$; throughout, $\tau$ is 10\% of the smallest critical dimension of the target (one-dimensional samples sit exactly at $\pm\tau$; two-dimensional samples are taken from a $\SI{2}{nm}$ grid within $\tau$ to $\tau+\SI{0.6}{nm}$ of the edge). Mask classes: $\mathcal M_\C=\C^F$; real masks $\mathcal M_\R=\{M:M_{-k}=\overline{M_k}\}$ (continuous-tone transmission in $[-1,1]$, the class containing phase-shift masks); nonnegative masks $\mathcal M_+\subset\mathcal M_\R$ (binary-like masks; an attenuated phase-shift mask with negative transmission belongs to $\mathcal M_\R$). The dark-to-bright ratio
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho}
\rho(M)=\frac{\max_{(x,z)\in D\times Z}\I_{M,z}(x)}{\min_{(x,z)\in B\times Z}\I_{M,z}(x)}\qquad(\rho:=+\infty\ \text{if the denominator vanishes})
\end{equation}
is scale-invariant. For a threshold resist (threshold $\theta$, dose $E$) every bright sample prints and no dark sample prints iff $\theta/\beta\le E<\theta/\delta$ with $\beta=\min_{B\times Z}\I$, $\delta=\max_{D\times Z}\I$; the full-range dose latitude, window width over its centre dose, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:el}
\EL(\rho)=\frac{2(1-\rho)}{1+\rho},
\end{equation}
with one dose over the whole focus set (the half-range value $(1-\rho)/(1+\rho)$ equals the Michelson contrast of the two extreme samples). The synthesis problem and its relaxation are
\begin{align}
(\mathrm P_{\mathcal M})\quad & \rhostar(\mathcal M)=\inf_{M\in\mathcal M\setminus\{0\}}\rho(M)\nonumber\\
 &\hphantom{\rhostar(\mathcal M)}= \inf\big\{t:\ M^{\mathsf H}Q_dM\le t\ \ \forall d\in D\times Z,\ \ M^{\mathsf H}Q_bM\ge1\ \ \forall b\in B\times Z\big\},\\
(\mathrm R)\quad & t_R=\min\big\{t:\ \Tr(Q_dX)\le t\ \ \forall d\in D\times Z,\ \ \Tr(Q_bX)\ge1\ \ \forall b\in B\times Z,\nonumber\\
 &\hphantom{t_R=\min\big\{t:\ }X\PSD\ \ [\,X=\conj(\Pi X\Pi)\,]\big\},\label{eq:R}
\end{align}
where $\Pi$ is the order-flip permutation and the bracketed constraint selects real masks.

Subscripted $\lambda_b$ is a dual weight; unsubscripted $\lambda$ is the wavelength. Table~\ref{tab:notation} collects the remaining symbols.

\section{Theory}\label{sec:theory}
\begin{theorem}[Certificate]\label{thm:cert}
Let $\mu\ge0$ on $D\times Z$ with $\sum\mu=1$, $\lambda\ge0$ on $B\times Z$, and $S(\mu,\lambda)=\sum_d\mu_dQ_d-\sum_b\lambda_bQ_b\PSD$. Then $\rho(M)\ge\sum_b\lambda_b$ for every $M\in\C^F\setminus\{0\}$ and for every incoherent sum of images $\sum_i\I_{M_i}$. For real masks it suffices that $\tilde S=\mathrm{Re}(U^{\mathsf H}SU)\PSD$ with $U$ a unitary basis of $\mathcal M_\R$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\beta=\min_{B\times Z}\I>0$ and $\delta=\max_{D\times Z}\I$. Then $\delta=\delta\sum_d\mu_d\ge\sum_d\mu_dM^{\mathsf H}Q_dM=M^{\mathsf H}SM+\sum_b\lambda_bM^{\mathsf H}Q_bM\ge\beta\sum_b\lambda_b$. For sums of images apply the same chain termwise. For $M=Ur$, $r\in\R^F$, $M^{\mathsf H}SM=r^{\mathsf T}\tilde Sr$.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[Real structure]\label{lem:real}
$J(M)=\conj(\Pi M)$ is an antilinear involution; $\mathcal M_\R=\mathrm{Fix}(J)$ is a real $F$-dimensional subspace on which the Hermitian form is real. A PSD $X$ satisfies $X=\conj(\Pi X\Pi)$ iff $XJ=JX$; then $X=UYU^{\mathsf H}$ with $Y\in\mathbb S^F_+$ and $\Tr(QX)=\Tr(\tilde QY)$, $\tilde Q=\mathrm{Re}(U^{\mathsf H}QU)$. Hence (R) for real masks is a real SDP of the same size.
\end{lemma}
\begin{theorem}[Relaxation equals unlimited multiple exposure]\label{thm:me}
For $\mathcal M\in\{\mathcal M_\C,\mathcal M_\R\}$, $t_R$ equals the optimal ratio of the summed image of any number of incoherently superposed exposures of masks in $\mathcal M$ printed with one common threshold. If (R) has a rank-one optimum then $\rhostar=t_R$ is attained by a single mask; conversely, if $\rhostar=t_R$ is attained then (R) has a rank-one optimum. (R) attains its optimum, and an extreme optimal solution has $\rank X=r$ with $r(r+1)/2\le|D\times Z|+|B\times Z|$ for $\mathcal M_\R$ and $r^2\le|D\times Z|+|B\times Z|$ for $\mathcal M_\C$; at most that many exposures are needed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$X\PSD\iff X=\sum_{i\le F}M_iM_i^{\mathsf H}$, and $\Tr(QX)=\sum_i M_i^{\mathsf H}QM_i$ is the summed image; Lemma~\ref{lem:real} gives real $M_i$ in the real-structured case. Attainment: the feasible set is closed; along a minimizing sequence the constraints bound $\Tr X$ on the orthogonal complement of the common null space of all $Q$'s, and $X$ may be set to zero on that null space without changing any constraint, so a convergent subsequence exists. Writing the inequalities with slacks, (R) is a standard-form SDP with $m=|D\times Z|+|B\times Z|$ equalities and block-diagonal cone $(X,\text{slacks},t^+,t^-)$; Pataki's theorem \cite{pataki1998} bounds $\sum_{\text{blocks}}r_b(r_b+1)/2\le m$ for real symmetric blocks and $\sum_b r_b^2\le m$ for Hermitian blocks.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
Attainment of $\rhostar$ in $(\mathrm P_{\mathcal M})$ is not automatic: $\rho$ is scale-invariant and continuous on the unit sphere away from $\{\min_B\I=0\}$, and the infimum may be approached by masks whose bright and dark intensities vanish together; $\rhostar$ is nevertheless certified by Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}. Theorem~\ref{thm:me} models double or multiple exposure of one resist with a common development threshold; Boolean multi-patterning (litho--etch--litho--etch) is a different process and is not bounded by $t_R$.
\end{remark}
\begin{theorem}[Nonnegative masks in one dimension]\label{thm:toeplitz}
For $d=1$, $K=\{-K_{\max},\dots,K_{\max}\}$ and $M\in\mathcal M_\R$, $M$ is the in-band coefficient vector of a nonnegative measure iff the Hermitian Toeplitz matrix $T(M)=(M_{a-b})_{a,b=0}^{K_{\max}}\PSD$; if $T(M)\succ0$ it is realized by a bounded, strictly positive continuous function. Hence $\overline{\mathcal M_+}=\{M:T(M)\PSD\}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Necessity: $v^{\mathsf H}T(M)v=|\Lambda|^{-1}\int m(x)|\sum_av_ae^{2\pi iax/L}|^2dx\ge0$. Sufficiency is the Carath\'eodory--Toeplitz theorem \cite{grenander1958,dumitrescu2007}; measures are weak-$*$ limits of bounded nonnegative functions (Fej\'er means). For $T(M)\succ0$ the Levinson--Durbin recursion gives the maximum-entropy autoregressive extension $f(\theta)=\varsigma^2/|a(e^{i\theta})|^2$ (a rational, not polynomial, density; $\varsigma^2$ is the innovation variance) with $a$ zero-free on the closed disk, matching $M_0,\dots,M_{K_{\max}}$; $f$ is continuous, bounded and bounded away from zero, and scaling it to $\max f=1$ does not change $\rho$.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
In two dimensions block-Toeplitz positivity is necessary but not sufficient for the truncated trigonometric moment problem \cite{dumitrescu2007}; it still yields a valid relaxation.
\end{remark}
\begin{proposition}[Cutoff]\label{prop:cutoff}
If no two collected orders pass the pupil for a common source point---for $d=1$ exactly when $L(\NA+\max_j|s_j|)/\lambda<1$, i.e.\ the pitch is below $\lambda/((1+\sigma)\NA)$; for $d=2$ when $\max(L_1,L_2)(\NA+\max_j|s_j|)/\lambda<1$---then $Q_{x,z}$ is independent of $x$, $t_R=1$, and uniform $(\mu,\lambda)$ is a certificate.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
With a single order per source point the image is $\sum_jw_j|M_{k_j}|^2$, independent of $x$; uniform weights give $S=0$ and $\sum_b\lambda_b=1$.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
The converse fails for some targets: just above the cutoff only the orders $0,\pm1$ interfere and the image is a sinusoid at the cell period, so a target whose bright and dark samples cannot be separated by such a sinusoid (e.g.\ $B=\{0,L/2\}$, $D=\{L/4,3L/4\}$) still has $t_R=1$. For dense lines $t_R<1$ immediately above the cutoff, and for two-beam imaging of dense lines at pitch $p$ the bound equals the closed form $(1-\sin(2\pi\tau/p))/(1+\sin(2\pi\tau/p))$ (verified to $2\times10^{-5}$ in the test suite).
\end{remark}
\begin{lemma}[Complex versus real masks]\label{lem:complex}
Let $w(-s)=w(s)$ and $P_0(-g)=\overline{P_0(g)}$ (even pupil amplitude, odd aberration phase). Then $\tilde M=\conj(\Pi M)$ produces the same image as $M$ at best focus, and with defocus $\I_{\tilde M,z}=\I_{M,-z}$. Consequently, for a focus set with $Z=-Z$ and no even aberrations, $t_R(\mathcal M_\C)=t_R(\mathcal M_\R)$ (take $X=\tfrac12(MM^{\mathsf H}+\tilde M\tilde M^{\mathsf H})$). Even aberrations (astigmatism, spherical, $Z_4$) break the identity.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Substituting $k\to-k$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:image} and using $\overline{P_z(-g)}=P_{-z}(g)$ and $w(-s)=w(s)$ gives $\I_{\tilde M,z}(x)=\I_{M,-z}(x)$; $X$ is real-structured since $\conj(\Pi MM^{\mathsf H}\Pi)=\tilde M\tilde M^{\mathsf H}$, and its constraint values are averages over $z\in Z=-Z$.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[Vector imaging and aberrations]\label{lem:vector}
With the image-side plane wave of direction cosines $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ carrying the mask-side polarization $p$ rotated by the minimal rotation taking $\hat z$ to the propagation direction (for $p=\hat x$: $E=(1-\alpha^2/(1+\gamma),\,-\alpha\beta/(1+\gamma),\,-\alpha)$, Rodrigues' formula; cf.\ \cite{flagello1996}), the image is a sum of rank-one Hermitian forms over channels (source point, polarization, field component), so Theorems~\ref{thm:cert}--\ref{thm:me} apply verbatim; Lemma~\ref{lem:complex} extends to reflection-symmetric polarization ensembles since $E_{x,y}(-g)=E_{x,y}(g)$, $E_z(-g)=-E_z(g)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{lemma}[Pixelation]\label{lem:pixel}
For a mask piecewise constant on $N\ge F$ centred pixels per period (one dimension), $M_k=\mathrm{sinc}(k/N)\,\mathrm{DFT}_k(m)$ maps onto $\C^F$, so the pixel-domain and Fourier-domain relaxations coincide for the scale-free ratio problem; $N<F$ can only raise the bound.
\end{lemma}
\begin{remark}[Sampled versus continuous constraints]
The continuous problem has more constraints than the sampled one, so a certificate for the sampled problem is a valid lower bound for the continuous problem; tightness is a separate question. Numerically the interior samples carry zero dual weight and the bound is invariant to the interior spacing (\S\ref{sec:robust}).
\end{remark}
\begin{theorem}[Single-mask lower bound by branch and bound]\label{thm:bnb}
Let $Q_1,\dots,Q_m\PSD$ (dark) and $P_1,\dots,P_n\PSD$ (bright) be real symmetric and $\rho(r)=\max_iq_i(r)/\min_jp_j(r)$. (i) $\rhostar(\mathcal M_\R)=\inf$ over the faces $\Phi_i=\{r:r_i=1,|r_j|\le1\}$. (ii) For a box $\{c+\delta:|\delta_j|\le h_j\}$ and $q(r)=r^{\mathsf T}Qr$ with $Q\PSD$: $q(c)-2|Qc|\cdot h\le q(r)\le q(c)+2|Qc|\cdot h+h^{\mathsf T}|Q|h$. (iii) Hence $\inf_{\text{box}}\rho\ge\mathrm{LB}:=\max_i\max(q_i(c)-2|Q_ic|\cdot h,0)/\min_j(p_j(c)+2|P_jc|\cdot h+h^{\mathsf T}|P_j|h)$, and a branch-and-bound over boxes covering $\bigcup_i\Phi_i$ returns, at any stopping time, a valid lower bound $L=\min_{\text{leaves}}\mathrm{LB}\le\rhostar(\mathcal M_\R)$ (leaves include the still-open boxes); on convergence $L\ge(1-\mathrm{tol})\,U$ with $U$ the incumbent.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) Scale and sign invariance of $\rho$: every $r\ne0$ has a coordinate of maximal modulus, which can be scaled to $+1$. (ii) $q(c+\delta)=q(c)+2(Qc)\cdot\delta+\delta^{\mathsf T}Q\delta$ with $0\le\delta^{\mathsf T}Q\delta\le h^{\mathsf T}|Q|h$. (iii) Interval arithmetic and the covering argument.
\end{proof}

\section{Algorithms}\label{sec:alg}
\paragraph{Bound.} Build $Q_{x,z}$ on the edge-offset sample set and solve the real SDP (CLARABEL \cite{clarabel}; SCS \cite{scs} as fallback).
\paragraph{Rigorization.} Extract the duals and let $\mathcal N$ be the common null space of $S_D=\sum_d\mu_dQ_d$ and $S_B=\sum_b\lambda_bQ_b$ (orders invisible to every weighted constraint; on $\mathcal N$ every form in Theorem~\ref{thm:cert} vanishes identically). Find by bisection the largest $\kappa\le1$ with $\lambda_{\min}(S_D-\kappa S_B)\ge10^{-10}\|S_D-\kappa S_B\|$ on $\mathcal N^\perp$ (if $S_B$ does not vanish on the near-null space of $S_D$, no projection is applied and the bisection lowers $\kappa$, to $0$ if needed), and report $\kappa\sum_b\lambda_b$. Independent check: a 50-digit Cholesky factorization of the projected matrix (mpmath). The forms $Q$ are double-precision images of exact complex exponentials (relative error $\sim10^{-15}$), five orders below the margin.
\paragraph{Primal.} Gaussian rounding from $X$; analytic-gradient L-BFGS on a log-sum-exp surrogate of $\log\rho$ (common dose across foci); vectorized SLSQP minimax polish; multistart; optimal-face exploration (random PSD objectives over the optimal face) for low-rank optima; explicit two-exposure refinement.
\paragraph{Single-mask certification.} (a) Theorem~\ref{thm:bnb} with batched boxes; error terms are multiplied by $1+10^{-9}$ and $10^{-12}$ is subtracted (a heuristic safeguard, not a proof of rounding control). (b) An SDP-bounded variant: to certify $\rhostar(\mathcal M_\R)\ge T$ the set $\{r\in\text{box}:\ r^{\mathsf T}(Q_d-TP_b)r\le0\ \forall(d,b)\}$ is shown empty through the Shor relaxation $[1,r^{\mathsf T};r,X]\PSD$ with reformulation--linearization box cuts, solved as ``minimize the common slack''; each node is pruned only if an explicit dual certificate $(y\ge0,\ \alpha,\ \beta)$ with $\sum_m y_mG_m+\alpha E_{00}+\beta E_{ii}\PSD$ and $\sum_my_mc_m-\alpha-\beta>0$ is verified after the solve (weak duality), so solver inaccuracy cannot produce a false pruning; restricting the pairs $(d,b)$ to the active supports of the global certificate keeps the relaxation valid. (c) Lasserre-2 moment relaxations \cite{lasserre2001} are reported as corroboration only (solver tolerance limited).
\paragraph{Variants.} SMO relaxation (one PSD block per source point: unlimited exposures with coherent point sources); doubly-nonnegative (DNN) relaxation for nonnegative masks on a pixel grid; complex-mask relaxation; multi-focus; vector and aberrated pupils; pixel-integrated masks.
\paragraph{Complexity.} $F\approx(2(1+\sigma)\NA L/\lambda)^d$; the SDP has an $F\times F$ matrix variable and $|B|+|D|$ constraints per focus. Solve times are below \sdpTimeSmallHi\,s for $F\le15$ and \sdpTimeBigLo--\sdpTimeBigHi\,s for $F=61$ with about 1,200 constraints.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}
\paragraph{Settings.} DUV: $\lambda=\SI{193}{nm}$, $\NA=1.35$, defocus in water ($n=1.44$); EUV: $\lambda=\SI{13.5}{nm}$, $\NA=0.33$, vacuum. Illuminators are area-weighted grid quadratures of the illuminator aperture: coherent (one on-axis point); conventional $\sigma\in\{0.5,0.7,0.9\}$ (disk, $21\times21$ grid, 349 points); dipole $\sigma_c\in\{0.7,0.8\}$ (two circular poles of radius $0.15\,\NA$ on the $x$ axis, 69 points each); annular 0.6/0.9 (DUV) and 0.5/0.9 (EUV) ($25\times25$ grid); quasar (four $30^\circ$ sectors of the annulus, $31\times31$ grid). The quadrature convergence of the bound is reported in \S\ref{sec:robust}. Edge tolerance $\tau$ is 10\% of the minimum CD (\SI{5}{nm} for the \SI{50}{nm} one-dimensional features). ``Bound'' is always the rigorized certificate value and ``best single mask'' the best feasible real mask found (\S\ref{sec:notation}); EL is the full-range value \eqref{eq:el}. All tables are generated from the clean reproduction.

\subsection{Source--mask entitlement}
For the iso-dense target the per-source-point relaxation (unlimited exposures, each with a coherent point source) equals the coherent-illumination relaxation (\smoCoh) both for the $\sigma=0.7$ disk (fixed-source bound \smoConvFixed) and for the $\sigma=0.9$ disk (\smoNineFixed); for the dipole the two coincide (\smoDipFixed). Thus no illumination supported on these points improves the \emph{multi-exposure} entitlement beyond on-axis coherent light. For a single exposure the statement is different: the best single coherent mask (Table~\ref{tab:1d}) is worse than the best single dipole mask, because the coherent relaxation is not tight.

\subsection{Adversarial one-dimensional targets}\label{sec:adv}
Near-cutoff mixed pitches, SRAF-like \SI{20}{nm} spaces, 5\%/95\% duty cycles, alternating widths and three-pitch mixtures (Fig.~\ref{fig:dataset}, middle; $\tau=10\%$ CD) are tight under dipole illumination, mostly tight under the $\sigma=0.7$ disk, and show gaps of \advCohGapLo--\advCohGapHi{} in \advCohBigN{} of six coherent cases, the regime in which multiple exposure helps most. The isolated \SI{20}{nm} space under coherent or dipole light has a certified bound of $\approx0$: a phase-edge mask makes the sampled dark points exactly dark. This is a statement about the sampled constraint set, not about the printed width of a phase-edge line, which is dose-determined.

\subsection{Robustness, solver validation and physical sensitivity}\label{sec:robust}
The bound is invariant to the interior sample spacing (\spacingMin--\spacingMax{} for spacings of 16 to \SI{1}{nm}) and monotone in $\tau$ (full-range EL: \marginList). Quadrature convergence of the $\sigma=0.7$ disk: \quadList{} (last two values differ by \quadLastTwoDiff). The Rayleigh-quotient bound is \rayLo--\rayHi$\times$ weaker (Table~\ref{tab:rayleigh}), and complex- and real-mask relaxations coincide to $\cplxMaxDiff$ on all \rayN{} cases (Lemma~\ref{lem:complex}). All \verN{} bounds of the verification set were recomputed and their projected certificate matrices factorized exactly by a 50-digit Cholesky (\verChol{} succeed, \verDeg{} degenerate with $S=0$, \verFail{} fail); the rigorization scale is at least \verKappaMin{} and below $0.9999$ in \verKappaBelow{} instances. CLARABEL and SCS agree to a median of $\verSolverMedian$; on \verBigDiffN{} near-degenerate instances (bounds up to \verBigDiffBoundMax) the first-order solver SCS is off by up to \verBigDiffMax, which the certificate check exposes. Under the vector model ($n_{\mathrm{resist}}=1.7$, Fig.~\ref{fig:bnb}, right) the one-dimensional TE bounds change by \vecTeLo{} to \vecTeHi{}, the unpolarized bounds by \vecUnLo{} to \vecUnHi{} and the TM bounds by \vecTmLo{} to \vecTmHi{} relative to the scalar model (the classical TM contrast loss at $\NA=1.35$), with gaps at most \vecGapMax; on the two-dimensional contacts the unpolarized bounds rise by \vecTwoDUnLo--\vecTwoDUnHi{} and the TE bounds by \vecTwoDTeLo--\vecTwoDTeHi. The vector model omits Fresnel transmission and thin-film effects, which rescale channel amplitudes without changing the structure of Lemma~\ref{lem:vector}. \section{Discussion}
The certificates turn folklore into checkable statements. Local optimization of the exposure-latitude objective is globally optimal for most one-dimensional targets and for the two-dimensional contact arrays; when it is not, the shortfall is---by Theorem~\ref{thm:me}---the benefit of incoherent multiple exposure, which the branch-and-bound certificates prove to be strictly positive on concrete coherent-illumination targets. On-axis coherent light with real masks is the multi-exposure entitlement for the iso-dense target, and nonnegative masks forfeit a certified \binPenLo--\binPenHi. Pattern-error ILT is far from EL-optimal, which argues for EL or contrast objectives, or for the certificate as a stopping criterion. The dual weights localize the limiting geometry. The structural conclusions survive the vector model and moderate aberrations; the numerical entitlements depend on the illuminator quadrature, the defocus medium and the edge tolerance, all of which are stated with each table. Finally, the relaxation gap has a physical meaning and is informative rather than merely a weakness.

\section{Limitations}
The model is a thin-mask periodic model: no three-dimensional mask electromagnetics (shadowing and best-focus shifts in EUV), no resist chemistry or stochastics, no flare, no mask error, and no dose or MEEF constraint (the ratio is scale-free; a dose-aware variant with $\Tr X\le1$ remains an SDP). Constraints are sampled with an explicit edge tolerance, and the EL objective fixes one threshold across the focus set; constraints linear in intensity (slope, dose-to-size) lift identically, but two-sided CD constraints and Boolean multi-patterning do not. The DNN relaxation is only an outer approximation in two dimensions and applies to pixelated nonnegative masks. ``Certified'' excludes a proof of rounding control. The interval branch-and-bound converges only for $F=5$ and is informative to $F=7$; the SDP-bounded variant certifies gains only away from the incumbent; one \SI{320}{nm} two-dimensional gap and the large-$F$ random two-dimensional gaps remain unresolved. Near-degenerate instances stress first-order solvers. Literature verification relied on abstracts and search snippets; the full texts of \cite{rosenbluth2002,rosenbluth2007,liu1992,nonaka2025,nouralishahi2008} were not accessible, so overlap with the body of those works cannot be excluded.
