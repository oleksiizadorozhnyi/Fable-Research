# Certified Bounds for Partially Coherent Mask Synthesis: Convex Printability Certificates, Rigorously Separated Single- and Multi-Exposure Optima, and Pattern-Specific Exposure-Latitude Limits

*Manuscript v2 (2026-10-11). Internally verified; not peer reviewed. All numbers are reproduced from clean runs of the
code in `code/` (see §9); tables are generated by `code/assemble.py` into `results/RESULTS_TABLES.md`.*

## Abstract
Mask synthesis for projection lithography (inverse lithography, ILT; source–mask optimization, SMO) is nonconvex, and
a local optimum carries no information about how far it is from the best achievable image. We show that, for the
Abbe/Hopkins model of a periodic cell—scalar or vector (high-NA, polarized), with arbitrary pupil aberrations and any
set of focus conditions—the best achievable dark-to-bright intensity ratio ρ* (equivalently the maximum exposure
latitude of a threshold resist) over all masks admits a semidefinite relaxation in the diffraction-order domain whose
dual is an elementary certificate: nonnegative weights on dark and bright sample points such that one explicit
matrix is positive semidefinite. The certificate is checked independently of the solver (an eigenvalue test; a
50-digit Cholesky factorization in our verification), is valid for the continuous constraint set when the sample
set contains the edge-offset points, and is made rigorous against solver round-off by a one-parameter bisection.
We prove that the relaxation equals the unlimited-multiple-exposure (incoherent superposition) problem, so its gap to
the best single mask is exactly the value of multiple exposure; that for symmetric illumination arbitrary complex
transmission cannot improve the relaxation; that in one dimension the collected spectra of nonnegative masks form the
Carathéodory–Toeplitz cone; and that mask pixelation at or above the collected-band count leaves the bound unchanged.
To separate single- from multi-exposure optima rigorously we introduce a branch-and-bound certifier for the
single-mask problem based on interval bounds of positive semidefinite quadratic forms; it converges in seconds for
five collected orders and certifies, for example, multi-exposure gains of 6.4–16.4% on random coherent-illumination
targets and ≥10.8% on a mixed-width target, independently of the Lasserre moment relaxation that we also report.
Across 109 random one-dimensional targets, local optimization is certified globally optimal in 70% of cases and within
1% in 83%; all two-dimensional contact arrays tested under annular, conventional and quasar illumination are certified
within 0.3%; 320 nm T-junction and line-end cells are certified within [[exp13]]. The bound reproduces the classical
k1 cutoffs exactly for DUV and EUV, is 3–150× tighter than the generalized-eigenvalue (Rayleigh-quotient) bound
underlying earlier global heuristics, certifies that binary masks forfeit ≥10% in ratio relative to phase-shift masks
on an iso-dense target, shows that a standard pattern-error ILT lands 30–67% above the certified optimum, and
survives the vector model: [[exp16 summary]].

## 1 Introduction
Computational lithography synthesizes mask and source so that the projected aerial image prints a target layout. In
the Abbe formulation the image is a weighted sum over source points of squared moduli of filtered mask spectra: it is
quadratic in the mask. ILT and SMO therefore optimize nonconvex functionals; IBM's wavefront-optimization work states
that mask optimization "tends inherently to be a problem with non-convex quadratic constraints" [2], and recent
work on learned ILT describes "highly non-convex objectives that often trap optimization in poor local minima" [13].
What is missing is a *certificate*: a proof, for a given target, illumination, numerical aperture and wavelength,
that no mask can do better than a stated value. Such certificates (i) separate unprintable layouts from unconverged
optimizers, (ii) benchmark ILT engines against the true optimum, (iii) quantify the value of multiple exposure and of
phase-shift versus binary masks, (iv) generalize the k1 rule to arbitrary layouts, and (v) expose which geometric
features (corners, line-ends) limit printability, through the support of the dual weights.

**Contributions.**
(C1) Theorem 1: dual certificates of printability valid for any mask class and for incoherent sums of masks, with a
one-matrix verification independent of the solver, and a rigorization procedure (§5.2).
(C2) Theorem 3: the relaxation equals the unlimited-multi-exposure problem; Pataki's bound limits the number of useful
exposures; Lemma 6: complex transmission cannot improve the relaxation under symmetric illumination; Theorem 4:
Carathéodory–Toeplitz realizability cone for nonnegative 1D masks; Lemma 7–8: vector imaging, aberrations and
pixelation preserve the structure.
(C3) A rigorous branch-and-bound certifier of the single-mask optimum (Algorithm 2), which, combined with (C1), proves
strictly positive multi-exposure gains on concrete targets.
(C4) An open pipeline and an empirical study: exact k1 cutoffs; pattern-specific exposure-latitude limits; certified
optimality gaps of local optimization, of standard ILT, and of binary versus phase-shift masks; 2D layouts under
annular/quasar illumination; robustness to sampling, solver, source quadrature, aberrations and the vector model.

**What is not claimed.** No new SDP algorithm (the Shor relaxation of a QCQP is textbook [4]); no full-chip
scalability; no thick-mask (3D) electromagnetics or resist chemistry; no statement about proprietary ILT engines.

## 2 Related work
*Wavefront optimization (IBM).* Rosenbluth et al. [1,2] optimize collected diffraction orders directly, maximizing
exposure latitude subject to bright/dark intensity constraints; a patent of the same group writes the problem as a
quadratic program in the order amplitudes with quadratic constraints [3]. Their global strategies partition the
solution space into regions with at most one local minimum when exposure latitude is expressed as edge-slope
maximization [2], use dominant "joint eigenvectors" and a "step-walk relaxation" [3], and, for multiple exposure, an
unconstrained Rayleigh-quotient simplification [3]. None of these yields a bound on solutions obtained by other means.
We compare directly with the Rayleigh-quotient bound (Table 7).
*Exact methods for small binary masks.* Liu and Zakhor [16] designed binary and phase-shift masks by branch and bound
on a pixel least-squares criterion; this is combinatorial and model-specific, whereas Algorithm 2 certifies the
continuous single-mask optimum of the ratio problem.
*SDP relaxation of QCQPs.* The Shor relaxation and its approximation theory are surveyed in [4]; the max-min multicast
beamforming problem [5] has exactly the structure of coherent-illumination mask synthesis (rank-one bright
constraints, interference-like dark constraints), so NP-hardness and randomization results transfer. Pataki [6]
bounds the rank of extreme solutions. Lasserre's hierarchy [14] tightens the bound for small problems.
*Bounds in photonic design.* Angeris, Vučković and Boyd [7] bound inverse-designed photonic devices by Lagrangian
duality and certify near-optimality of local designs; our work is the imaging-side analogue. SDP relaxations with
randomized rounding have been used in lithography only for the combinatorial mask-assignment problem of multiple
patterning [17], not for aerial-image synthesis.
*Information-theoretic limits.* Ma, Arce, Garcia-Frias et al. [8] bound OPC fidelity through mutual information; these
are not layout-specific duality certificates.
*Lifting in imaging.* PhaseLift [9] lifts quadratic measurements for recovery; we lift for synthesis.

## 3 Model and problem
(Full definitions: `notes/THEORY.md` §1–2.) A periodic cell Λ with mask transmission m has collected orders M ∈ ℂ^F
(|f_k| ≤ (NA + max_j|s_j|)/λ). For source points s_j with weights w_j, focus z, pupil phase W, and (optionally) the
vector channels of Lemma 7, the image is I_{M,z}(x) = Σ_c w_c |c_{c,x,z}^H M|² = M^H Q_{x,z} M with Q_{x,z} ⪰ 0.
Bright/dark sample sets B, D are placed at exactly ±τ (edge tolerance) from every target edge plus interior points;
Z is a focus set. The ratio ρ(M) = max_{D×Z} I / min_{B×Z} I is scale-invariant and EL(ρ) = (1−ρ)/(1+ρ) is the
exposure latitude of a threshold resist. Problem (P_𝓜): ρ*(𝓜) = inf ρ(M) over a class (complex 𝓜_ℂ, real 𝓜_ℝ,
nonnegative 𝓜_+). Relaxation (R): min t s.t. Tr(Q_{x,z}X) ≤ t on D×Z, ≥ 1 on B×Z, X ⪰ 0 [, X = conj(ΠXΠ)].

## 4 Theory
Statements and proofs are in `notes/THEORY.md`; we summarize.
- **Theorem 1 (certificate).** μ ≥ 0 on D×Z, Σμ = 1, λ ≥ 0 on B×Z and S = Σμ_d Q_d − Σλ_b Q_b ⪰ 0 imply ρ(M) ≥ Σλ_b
  for every M, and for every incoherent sum of images. (Three-line weak duality.)
- **Lemma 2.** Real masks form a real F-dimensional subspace; the real-structured PSD cone is isometric to 𝕊^F_+.
- **Theorem 3.** t_R equals the optimum of the unlimited-multi-exposure problem; single-mask attainment ⇔ a rank-one
  optimum; extreme optima have rank r with r(r+1)/2 ≤ |D×Z|+|B×Z|+1.
- **Theorem 4.** In 1D, in-band spectra of nonnegative masks = {M : Toeplitz(M) ⪰ 0}; strictly positive-definite
  spectra are realized by bounded positive densities (maximum-entropy/Fejér–Riesz extension).
- **Proposition 5.** Below pitch λ/((1+σ)NA) the bound is exactly 1 with the uniform certificate.
- **Lemma 6.** Symmetric illumination ⇒ complex-mask relaxation = real-mask relaxation.
- **Lemma 7–8.** Vector imaging and aberrations preserve the quadratic structure; pixelation with N ≥ F pixels leaves
  the bound unchanged.
- **Sampling.** A certificate for the sampled problem is valid for the continuous problem (more constraints).
- **Theorem 9 (single-mask B&B certificate).** For PSD Q and a box {c + δ : |δ_j| ≤ h_j}, q(c) − 2|Qc|·h ≤ q ≤
  q(c) + 2|Qc|·h + h^T|Q|h; since ρ is invariant to scale and sign, the faces {r_i = 1, |r_j| ≤ 1} cover all real masks,
  and a branch-and-bound over these faces with LB(box) = max_z max_d q_d^lo / min_b q_b^hi returns a value L with
  ρ*(𝓜_ℝ) ≥ L whenever it terminates (converged or not). Proof: PSD ⇒ δ^TQδ ≥ 0; the rest is interval arithmetic and
  the covering argument. Floating-point: error terms inflated by 1+1e-9 and L reduced by 1e-12; Q entries carry
  O(1e-16) relative rounding, several orders below the reported gaps. ∎

## 5 Algorithms
5.1 Bound: build Q (F×F) on the edge-offset sample set; solve the real SDP (CLARABEL; SCS fallback).
5.2 Rigorization: extract duals; find the largest c ≤ 1 with Σμ Q_d − cΣλ Q_b ⪰ 0 on the orthogonal complement of the
near-null space of Σμ Q_d (orders invisible to all constraints), by bisection on the concave minimum eigenvalue; verify
that Σλ Q_b vanishes on that space (residual reported); report cΣλ. Independent check: 50-digit Cholesky.
5.3 Primal: Gaussian rounding from X; analytic-gradient L-BFGS on a log-sum-exp surrogate; vectorized SLSQP minimax
polish; multistart; optimal-face exploration (random PSD objectives over the optimal face) for low-rank optima; explicit
two-exposure refinement of the top-2 eigenvectors (Table 8).
5.4 Single-mask certification: Lasserre-2 moment relaxation (F ≤ 11; solver-tolerance limited) and Algorithm 2
(rigorous B&B; converges for F = 5 in < 2 s, gives valid partial bounds for F = 7–11).
5.5 Variants: SMO relaxation (one PSD block per source point); DNN relaxation for nonnegative masks (pixel domain);
complex-mask relaxation; multi-focus; vector/aberrated pupils; pixel-integrated masks.
Complexity: F ≈ (2(1+σ)NA L/λ)^d; SDP in F×F with |B|+|D| constraints per focus; 0.1–1.4 s for F ≤ 15, 100–200 s for
F = 61 (1,200 constraints) with CLARABEL.

## 8 Limitations
Scalar/vector thin-mask periodic model (no 3D mask electromagnetics, resist chemistry, flare, mask error); sampled
constraints with an explicit edge tolerance; ratio/EL objective (any constraint linear in intensity lifts identically;
EPE enters only through τ); binary-mask relaxation is an outer approximation in 2D; Lasserre-2 values carry solver
status; B&B converges only for small F (partial bounds otherwise); 320 nm 2D gaps not certified as multi-exposure
gains unless Table 8 shows a two-exposure realization attaining the bound; literature coverage limited to search
snippets (full texts of [1,2,16] not read).
