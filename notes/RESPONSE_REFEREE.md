# Response to the referee report (major revision), 2026-10-12

We thank the referee for a careful and substantive report. Every item led to a change in the manuscript, the code,
or both. Numbers quoted below are regenerated by `code/make_numbers.py` from the final clean reproduction.

## 1. Rounding safety of the numerical certificates
**Change.** The abstract, (C1) and §5 no longer describe the certificates as "safe against round-off". Instead, every
reported certificate is now re-derived exactly: `code/exp21_exact_verify.py` REBUILDS the certificate matrix
S~ = Re U^H (Σ μ_d Q_d − κ Σ λ_b Q_b) U in 50-digit arithmetic from the model equations (pupil, defocus, Fourier phases)
and the instance data (cell, λ, NA, index, focus set, source points and weights, sample coordinates — all exact as the
double-precision numbers defining the instance), using the duals (μ, λ) and the scale κ found in double precision, and
Cholesky-factorizes S~ − 10^-30 I at 50 digits. This proves λ_min(S~) ≥ 10^-30 up to the 50-digit rounding error
(≲ F² 10^-48 ‖S~‖), so Theorem 1 applies to the stated discrete instance. The minimum eigenvalue of the rebuilt matrix
is reported (it is of the order of the double-precision margin, 10^-10, showing that the margin was not a cancellation
artefact). The previous check (50-digit Cholesky of the matrix assembled in double precision) is kept as a secondary
check and described as such. The double-precision bisection is now described as a heuristic safeguard. What remains
unproved is stated: no interval arithmetic; no statement about other discretizations. The instance data and duals are
archived (`results/certs/*.npz`).

## 2. Theorem 4 (nonnegative masks and measures)
**Change.** Theorem 4 now distinguishes three sets: {T ≻ 0} ⊂ C_+ (in-band spectra of bounded nonnegative masks) ⊂
{T ⪰ 0} = {moments of nonnegative measures}, both inclusions strict, with the referee's point-mass example
(M_0 = M_±1 = a) given in the proof, cl(C_+) = {T ⪰ 0}, and the statement that the relaxation over {T ⪰ 0} is an outer
approximation with the same optimal value as the nonnegative-mask problem (proved by perturbing M_0 by ε and continuity
of ρ where bright intensities are positive), which need not be attained by a bounded mask. The max-entropy AR extension
is kept as the realization for T ≻ 0.

## 3. Symmetric order set for real masks
**Change.** §3.1 now states that an order k is discarded only if neither k nor −k is brought into the pupil by any
source point, so K = −K always; a dead order whose mirror is collected stays in K as an unconstrained coordinate tied
to its mirror by M_{−k} = conj(M_k). This is what the code does (`Model.__init__`), and it preserves the real
F-dimensional structure of Lemma 2 (in the referee's example K = {−1, 0, 1}, F = 3 real degrees of freedom).

## 4. Proposition 5
**Change.** The "exactly when" clause is removed. The hypothesis is now "for every source point at most one collected
order passes"; for d = 1 the exact passing condition |k/L + s_x/λ| ≤ sqrt(NA² − s_y²)/λ is stated, the exact criterion
(no two integers in any of the per-source intervals) and the sufficient condition L max_j(|s_jx| + sqrt(NA² − s_jy²)) < λ
(the referee's quantity; equal to the classical cutoff for disk illumination) are given, and the referee's
counter-example to the radial condition is included. The d = 2 condition is stated as sufficient.

## 5. Phase-shift advantage and ILT suboptimality
**Change.** The comparison of two lower bounds is removed. `code/exp22_psm_penalty.py` computes the certified bracket
L_+/U_R − 1 ≤ ρ*_+/ρ*_R − 1 ≤ U_+/L_R − 1 with U_R the best feasible PSM ratio and U_+ the best feasible nonnegative
pixel mask (bounded L-BFGS from eight starts), on the same sample sets. Table 5 reports L_R, U_R, L_+, U_+, the penalty
bracket and the ILT bracket [ρ_ILT/U_R − 1, ρ_ILT/L_R − 1]; the abstract and Discussion quote lower and upper bounds.
(With the previous data this gave 4.8 %, 10.2 % and 2.7 % certified lower bounds, as the referee computed.)

## 6. Relaxation gap versus multiple-exposure benefit
**Change.** The Discussion and a new remark after Theorem 3 state that a gap between a local-search mask and t_R is an
upper bound on the multi-exposure benefit that may contain optimizer shortfall; a strict benefit is claimed only where a
certified single-mask lower bound exceeds the ratio of a feasible ensemble (converged branch-and-bound runs), and the
unresolved gaps are described as "not evidence either way". "Certified globally optimal" is replaced by "certified
optimal to within 10^-4".

## 7. Attainment in Theorem 3 and for single masks
**Change.** The incorrect compactness argument is replaced by Lemma (Attainment): the problem is reduced to the
orthogonal complement of the common dark null space N; bright forms vanishing on N give a reduced SDP whose dual is
strictly feasible (Σ_d Q_d is positive definite on N^⊥), so the reduced primal attains (Vandenberghe–Boyd 1996); forms
not vanishing on N are satisfied by adding c(I − P) without changing dark or reduced-bright values. The same decomposition
proves attainment of ρ* for single masks (the referee's argument: generic large component in N, lower semicontinuity of
the reduced ratio on the compact sphere), and the earlier remark claiming possible non-attainment is removed. The
hypothesis "every bright form is nonzero" is stated (otherwise ρ* = t_R = +∞).

## 8. SDP branch-and-bound thresholds
**Change.** The thresholds were stale values from an earlier instance definition; they are now chosen strictly between
the ensemble ratio and the incumbent (iso-dense coherent: 0.56, 0.60; mixed coherent: 0.60, 0.62; three-focus mixed
edge-only: 0.84), the text explains the admissible range, and the outcomes are reported as certified / not terminated.

## 9. Inconsistencies
**Change.** Table 6 now states the actual hole widths (70, 50, 44 nm at pitches 160, 120, 100 nm; τ = 7, 5, 4.4 nm).
Figure 1's caption says that bound and best mask agree within the reported ratio gap and refers to Table 2 for both.
The abstract and §6.3 use the same denominator (non-degenerate instances) and name the excluded near-degenerate instance.

## 10. EL brackets
**Change.** Tables 1, 2 and 6 now carry an "EL (best)" column next to "Max EL", and the EUV text gives the EL bracket for
the 22 nm case explicitly, noting that near ρ = 1 a few-percent ratio gap brackets EL loosely.

## 11. Discretization uncertainty
**Change.** (a) The source quadrature is refined from a 21² to a 401² midpoint grid (≈126,000 points for a disk; the
coherent-mode compression of the source sum, now built directly from the pupil vectors, makes this free), which
converges the bound to ≈10^-4; the convergence table extends to an 801² grid. (b) §6.9 states that every certificate is a
statement about the stated discrete illuminator, sample set and focus set, that only an estimate (not a bound) is
available for the continuous illuminator, that a sampled-problem bound remains valid for the continuous geometry
(more constraints) while tightness statements hold only for the sampled constraints, and that zero interior dual weight
does not exclude violations between samples.

## 12. Reproducibility and prior art
**Change.** The Data and Code Availability section names the repository URL and lists what is archived: sample
coordinates and labels, order sets, source points/weights, focus sets, duals (μ, λ), κ and bounds for every verified
instance (`results/certs/`), best masks, lifted solutions (ensembles), duals of the main experiments, seeds, and
`results/environment.txt` with Python/numpy/scipy/cvxpy/CLARABEL/SCS/mpmath versions. Table 2's caption states the EUV
cell (four pitches, L = 4 × pitch; hence F = 7 at 20 nm). The prior-art sentence is narrowed: the multiple-exposure
patent minimizes an averaged dark-to-bright objective, whose global minimum would bound the pointwise ratio only through
that weaker averaged quantity. Reference [2] now reads Haoyu Yang and Haoxing Ren, arXiv:2602.19027.
